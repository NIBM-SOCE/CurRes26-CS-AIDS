## 3. Module Purpose and Aims

The Capstone is where the pathway stops being a set of modules and becomes a capability. A student takes a real problem in the artificial-intelligence and data-science domain, scopes it themselves, chooses and justifies an approach, builds something that works, and then stands in front of people and defends what they did.

Its two functions should be read separately, because they pull in slightly different directions and the design has to serve both. The first is progression: NB6020CEM at the degree expects a student who can sustain independent work over months, and neither destination provides a research-preparation module to build that. The second is evidence: the artefact is a portfolio piece the graduate can show an employer, and a competency record the accrediting authority can inspect. The design resolves the tension by fixing the process, through the build log, the supervision pattern and the viva, while leaving the product open, so the artefact can be genuinely the student's own.

The honest limit: this is not degree-level independent research. It is supervised integrative practice with a defended outcome, which is the correct level for a Higher Diploma and the correct preparation for what follows.

**LO provenance note (MD only):** authored canonically here from the record set's indicative stubs under 08 section 3.2 and flagged for ratification. LO1 gains the self-defined scope, LO2 gains the justification clause, and LO3 is treated below.

**SLQF-04 position - LIFTED as a quality lift, not a compliance one (thread ruling, and a scope question for master).** The cluster-wide rule adopted at v8.1 Phase 2 requires that every Level 4 **taught** module carry at least one analytical outcome. This is a Pathway / Skills module, so the rule does not reach it and no lift is mandated. Two observations follow, and the second is the substantive one:

- As stubbed, this module's outcomes were **plan, apply and demonstrate**, none of them analytical. LO3 is lifted to an evaluative formulation because the viva-defence in the common frame already demands exactly that: a student who cannot evaluate their own outcome against the problem cannot defend it. The lift makes the assessment honest rather than adding scope.
- **Raised for master:** the analytical rule is scoped to taught modules, so the Capstone and Industrial Training were never audited against it. That is defensible for Industrial Training, whose competencies are workplace rather than cognitive. It is harder to defend for the Capstone, which carries the most integrative and most publicly visible work in the programme and which feeds a degree-level project module. Whether Pathway / Skills modules should be brought inside the rule is a cross-pathway question and is escalated, not settled here.

## 5. Skills Programme Structure

All figures are the Profile C distribution and are fixed; the totals close at 250.

### Briefings and methods input (15 hours, five 3-hour blocks)

| Block | Theme |
|---|---|
| B1 | Scoping: from an interest to a defensible problem statement, and what makes a scope deliverable in the hours available |
| B2 | Methods for the pathway: selecting an approach across the modelling, engineering and reasoning strands, and justifying the selection |
| B3 | Data, ethics and responsible use: provenance, consent, licensing, and the limits of what a project may claim |
| B4 | Building and evidencing: build-log discipline, reproducibility, and version control as evidence rather than as habit |
| B5 | Communicating and defending: the demonstration, the report, and preparation for the viva |

### Supervision (21 hours)

Individual supervision totalling 21 hours per student: an initial scoping meeting at which the problem statement is agreed, followed by regular scheduled meetings across the project. The distribution pattern is set at specification stage; the total is fixed. Supervision is a record as well as a support: meeting notes form part of the evidence that the work is the student's own.

### Laboratory and build access (39 hours, supervised)

Scheduled supervised access to laboratory and build facilities. This is a resourcing commitment with a cost that should be visible: 39 supervised hours per student, multiplied by cohort size, is a substantial timetabled demand, and it is the component most likely to come under pressure. If it is reduced, the build log thins and the artefact quality with it.

### Demonstration and viva (9 hours)

The demonstration session at which the artefact is presented and exercised, and the viva at which the report is defended. Both are scheduled events, not informal reviews.

### Independent project work (166 hours)

Not session-scheduled. This is the largest component by design and is where the project is actually done.

## 6. Assessment

Components per the Assessment Component Taxonomy (04_Conventions.md v1.2) and the common frame; weightings confirmed at specification sign-off.

| Component code | Task | Attributes | ILOs | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CW-I-Home | Project artefact with build log: a working integrative deliverable in the pathway domain, accompanied by a build log evidencing the development process, the decisions taken and the points at which the approach changed | Individual; take-home (unsupervised); graded; oral vehicle: Presentation at demonstration | LO1, LO2, LO3 | 60 |
| CW-I-Home | Final report: the problem, the approach and its justification, the outcome, and an evaluation of the outcome against the problem, including what the project does not establish | Individual; take-home (unsupervised); graded; oral vehicle: **Viva-defence** (mandatory) | LO1, LO2, LO3 | 40 |

**Setting-attribute note (MD only):** both components are coded take-home. The common frame's phrase "supervised access" describes the 39 timetabled laboratory hours, which are a delivery arrangement, not an assessment condition. Coding either component as in-class would misrepresent the conditions under which the work is actually produced, and the distinction is recorded here so that it is not lost at specification stage.

**No examination is carried**, per the common frame and by design. The capability certified is an integrative built outcome and the ability to defend it, neither of which an examination measures.

**Reassessment** is by failed component. **Academic integrity and generative AI:** the build log and the viva-defence are jointly the authorship mechanism. Generative-AI assistance must be declared and located in the build log at the point it was used. A student who cannot account for their own artefact and its development at the viva cannot pass, irrespective of the artefact's quality: this is stated plainly because an integrative project is the single most exposed assessment in the programme to undeclared generation.
