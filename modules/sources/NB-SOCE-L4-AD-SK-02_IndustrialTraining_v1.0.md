## 3. Module Purpose and Aims

This module puts the student inside a working organisation for approximately twelve weeks and holds them to professional standards there. It is the only place in the programme where competence is demonstrated in conditions the School does not control, against work the School did not set, judged by people who are not academics.

That is precisely its value, and it is why the evidence it generates is different in kind from everything else in the award. A taught module can evidence that a student can do something; a placement evidences that they did it, in an organisation, to that organisation's satisfaction. For a programme pursuing vocational accreditation, this module carries the weight.

The design consequence is that the module's academic apparatus is deliberately light and its evidence discipline is deliberately heavy. There is no syllabus. There are three artefacts, and each has a job: the log records what was actually done while it is still accurate, the report makes sense of it, and the viva confirms that the student can account for both.

The honest limit: placement quality varies, and the School controls the preparation, the touchpoints and the assessment but not the work. The touchpoint schedule exists to catch a placement that is not delivering while there is still time to act.

## 5. Skills Programme Structure

All figures are the Profile D distribution and are fixed; the totals close at 500.

### Induction and placement preparation (9 hours, three 3-hour blocks)

| Block | Theme |
|---|---|
| I1 | Placement expectations and conduct: the student's obligations to the host, the School's obligations to both, and the escalation route when a placement is not working |
| I2 | The evidence discipline: what the log must record, when it must be written, and why a log reconstructed afterwards is worthless as evidence |
| I3 | Professional practice in an employment setting: confidentiality and data handling, workplace safety, and conduct expectations |

### Placement (458 hours)

Approximately twelve weeks full-time, sourced either externally or through internal placement provision. **Scheduled outside teaching weeks**, per the unalterable commitment reproduced in section 2. Work is assigned by the host organisation; the School does not set it and does not assess it directly, but does hold it against the placement agreement at each touchpoint.

### Supervision touchpoints (12 hours, four 3-hour touchpoints)

Scheduled contact across the placement, structured so that an early touchpoint can identify a placement not delivering against the agreement while there is still time to intervene. Touchpoints are records as well as support: they evidence continuous School oversight, which is the assurance a vocational accreditation regime will look for.

### Reporting and assessment (21 hours)

Preparation and submission of the placement log and report, and the closing viva.

## 6. Assessment

Components per the Assessment Component Taxonomy (04_Conventions.md v1.2) and the common frame; weightings confirmed at specification sign-off.

| Component code | Task | Attributes | ILOs | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PR-I-Class (provisional, see finding) | Placement completion: completion of the placement to the terms of the placement agreement, confirmed by the host organisation and the School | Individual; supervised (workplace); **Pass-Fail** | LO1, LO2 | Pass-Fail, no percentage weight; a condition of completing the module |
| CW-I-Home | Individually maintained placement log, written contemporaneously across the placement and recording tasks undertaken, competencies exercised and supervision received | Individual; take-home (unsupervised); graded; oral vehicle: None | LO1, LO3 | 30 |
| CW-I-Home | Placement report: an account of the organisation, the work undertaken, the competencies evidenced, and a reflection against the competencies the placement was intended to evidence | Individual; take-home (unsupervised); graded; oral vehicle: **Viva-defence** (mandatory, closing) | LO1, LO2, LO3 | 70 |

Graded weights sum to 100. The placement-completion component carries no percentage weight and is a threshold condition.

**Pass-Fail justification (required by the common frame).** Placement completion is graded Pass-Fail because it is a threshold competency claim, not a performance to be ranked: either the student completed a supervised placement to the terms agreed or they did not, and a percentage grade would imply a comparability across host organisations that does not exist. The taxonomy expressly reserves Pass-Fail for threshold competency-evidence components, and this is one. Grading it would also weaken the evidence: an auditable claim that a placement was completed is stronger than a mark whose basis varies by employer.

**Taxonomy findings raised for master (both cross-pathway, since every pathway carries this module).**

1. **No setting value fits a workplace placement.** The taxonomy's setting axis offers In-class (supervised) and Take-home (unsupervised). A placement is supervised, but not by the School and not in class. The provisional encoding above uses PR-I-Class and reads "in-class (supervised)" as "supervised", with the attribute string naming host-organisation supervision. That is a stretch of a locked vocabulary, and threads may not extend attribute values locally. **Recommendation:** a change request adding a workplace-supervised setting value.
2. **The closing Viva-defence cannot be a standalone component.** The common frame lists it as a fourth component, but the taxonomy holds oral vehicle as an attribute and expressly routes any promotion of a standalone oral component to a primary type through master as a change request. It is therefore encoded above as the mandatory oral vehicle of the report component, which preserves its mandatory character and its weight without extending the type set. **Recommendation:** confirm this reading, or issue the change request the frame implies.

**No examination is carried**, per the common frame and by design.

**Reassessment** is by failed component, with the practical constraint recorded honestly: the log and report are reassessable, and placement completion is not reassessable within the same placement. A failed completion requires a further placement, which is a scheduling and provision question rather than an assessment one.

**Academic integrity and generative AI:** the log is contemporaneous evidence and its value depends entirely on its being written as the work happened. Generative assistance in composing a log after the fact defeats the artefact's purpose and is treated as a breach rather than an undeclared aid. The viva is where the log and report are tested against the student's actual recall of the placement.

### Governance finding raised for master (D1)

This module carries no named Level 5 or 6 feeder. Its feeder statement is professional practice and employment, which is not a taught-content feeder claim, and it does not appear on the consolidated D1 exception register.

The likely correct position is that the module exists by D4, the standing decision carrying Capstone and Industrial Training, and that D4 pre-empts the D1 feeder question rather than the module being an unregistered exception. That reading is almost certainly right, but it is currently implicit: a reviewer applying D1 mechanically across the module set would flag this module and find no answer in the register. **Recommendation:** annotate the D1 exception register to record D4 as the standing authority for both Pathway / Skills modules, or state the position in the compliance narrative. This is a documentation gap rather than a design fault, and it costs one line to close.
