## 2. Module Purpose and Aims

This module treats visualisation as a design discipline grounded in how human perception actually works, not as a tour of chart types. Students learn why some encodings are read accurately and others are not, how a visualisation carries an argument, and how to judge whether a given visualisation answers the question it claims to answer, including their own.

Its scale must be stated honestly. At 2 credits and 10 lecture sessions this is the thinnest pathway module in the set, and it is thin for a recorded reason: it was re-banded down to fund the thirteenth Year 2 module, which now carries the temporal half of the same destination claim. The design response is to protect depth on perception and critique, which nothing else in the programme teaches, and to keep tool coverage deliberately shallow because the students arrive already able to produce a chart.

The scope limits are two. Statistical modelling belongs to Statistical Methods and Analysis, so a visualisation here presents structure rather than inferring it. Time-series and forecasting content, including the temporal visualisation of modelled series, belongs to Time Series and Forecasting, and that exclusion is stricter than it first appears: a student may plot an ordered series here as data, but the presentation of a fitted model and its forecast is the other module's outcome.

**LO provenance note (MD only):** authored canonically here from the record set's indicative stubs under 08 section 3.2 and flagged for ratification. LO1 and LO2 gain purpose and argument clauses. LO3 is treated below.

**SLQF-04 disposition for LO3 - LIFTED, and the second new finding raised (thread ruling, for master).** As with Statistical Methods and Analysis, this is not one of the five named lift obligations and this record was marked clean at the pathway verb-ladder audit. It is not clean:

- The stubbed set was **apply, construct and select and use**. None of those verbs sits in the Level 4 analytical column.
- This module does not appear on the audit's list of Year 2 records evidencing the analysis delta. The clean verdict and the evidence list disagree, in the same way and for the same reason as they do on Statistical Methods and Analysis.

The lift is made on the existing LO3 rather than by adding a fourth outcome, which would be disproportionate for a 2-credit module. The tool-selection content of the stub is retained in full and an evaluative clause is added. The addition is inside the boundary, since evaluating a visualisation against perceptual principles is the direct application of the covers line, and it is the natural analytical act of this discipline: critique is how visualisation is actually taught and assessed.

**Recommendation to master (restated from the Statistical Methods and Analysis descriptor, and now evidenced twice):** the pathway verb-ladder audit should be re-run against ratified outcome sets rather than stub sets. Two of the twenty-one records marked clean did not satisfy the cluster-wide Level 4 rule on inspection.

## 4. Curriculum and Delivery

### Indicative Syllabus

| Unit | Content | Lecture hours |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | Human perception and encoding: pre-attentive attributes, the encoding channels and their relative accuracy, and why the ranking of channels is an empirical result rather than a preference | 9 |
| U2 | Visual-design principles: chart selection for the question asked, scale and axis honesty, colour used as information rather than decoration, and decluttering | 9 |
| U3 | Data storytelling: narrative structure, audience, and the argument a visualisation is making whether or not its author intended one | 6 |
| U4 | Visualisation tools in practice: declarative and library-based approaches, and reproducibility of a figure | 3 |
| U5 | Critique and revision: reading a visualisation against the question it claims to answer, and revising one's own work on that basis | 3 |
| | **Total lecture hours** | **30** |

**Session note:** 30 lecture hours are delivered as 10 sessions of 3 hours (V10), with unit allocations in multiples of 3 so that unit boundaries fall on session boundaries.

**Allocation cost, named:** U4 receives a single session. Tool instruction is deliberately the thinnest strand, because students arrive from Applied Data Exploration already able to produce figures and because the destination assumes design judgment rather than tool coverage. If the module is later challenged as tool-light, that is the trade recorded here, and the alternative would have been to reduce the perception unit, which nothing else in the programme teaches.

**Standing exposure, named:** this is the thinnest pathway module in the set. It was re-banded from 3 credits to 2 to fund the thirteenth Year 2 module. The design above absorbs that reduction by cutting breadth rather than depth, but the module has no further slack, and any additional demand placed on it has to be met by a re-banding rather than by internal adjustment.

**Working-language note (V13 as amended by V14):** Python throughout for practical work, continuing the pathway language. Several standard texts in this field use R for their figures; the principles they teach are language-independent and the reading list states this where relevant.

## 5. Assessment

Components per the Assessment Component Taxonomy (04_Conventions.md v1.2); weightings confirmed at specification sign-off.

| Component code | Task | Attributes | ILOs | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CW-I-Home | Two-part portfolio. Part A: a set of visualisations built from a supplied dataset for a stated audience and question, presented as a short data story. Part B: a written critique of two published visualisations, evaluated against the question each claims to answer and the perceptual principles each relies on, with a suggested revision for one of them | Individual; take-home (unsupervised); graded; oral vehicle: Presentation (10 minutes at demonstration, delivering the data story and defending one design decision) | LO1, LO2, LO3 | 60 |
| PR-I-Class | Supervised laboratory assessment: build a small set of visualisations from an unseen dataset within a fixed session, with a brief written justification of the encoding choices made | Individual; in-class (supervised); graded | LO1, LO3 | 40 |

**No examination is carried by design.** The capability certified here is design judgment evidenced in produced work and in critique, neither of which an unseen written paper measures. The supervised laboratory assessment supplies the constrained individual evidence.

**Reassessment** is by failed component. **Academic integrity and generative AI:** the portfolio is an open-resource task in which generative-AI assistance must be declared; the critique must be the student's own reading rather than a generated one, and the presentation is where that is tested. The laboratory assessment is closed to such assistance.

## 6. Resources, Staff and Governance

### Indicative Reading

**Core**
- Munzner, T. (2014) *Visualization Analysis and Design*. A K Peters / CRC Press. Primary text for U1, U2 and U5; the analysis framework it provides is what makes critique teachable rather than a matter of taste.
- Wilke, C. O. (2019) *Fundamentals of Data Visualization: A Primer on Making Informative and Compelling Figures*. O'Reilly Media. ISBN 978-1-4920-3108-6. Second text for U2, freely available at clauswilke.com/dataviz. **Dependency stated:** the figures are produced in R; the principles and the directory of visualisation types are language-independent and no R competence is required or assessed.

**Supplementary**
- Knaflic, C. N. (2015) *Storytelling with Data: A Data Visualization Guide for Business Professionals*. Wiley. Used for U3. **Dependency stated:** a practitioner text rather than a research-grounded one; set for its narrative structure and paired with Munzner so that the storytelling strand remains anchored in perception.
- Tufte, E. R. (2001) *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*. 2nd edition. Graphics Press. Reference for the design tradition and the honesty arguments in U2. **Dependency stated:** predates current empirical perception research; cited for its arguments, not as current guidance.
- VanderPlas, J. (2022) *Python Data Science Handbook: Essential Tools for Working with Data*. 2nd edition. O'Reilly Media. Carried forward from Applied Data Exploration and used for U4 only, which is where the module's tool coverage sits.

**School-produced**
- A critique library of published visualisations with known design faults, an encoding-accuracy exercise set, and a data-story template. Named as School-produced and confirmed at teaching-plan stage.
